A tamper-evident black box for home-security events

After a break-in or a gas leak, the alarm log is the evidence — and on a cheap device anyone can edit it. CAIRN seals every event in a hash chain authenticated by HMAC-SHA256 under a forward-secure key ratchet, and tells you exactly which record was altered.

How it works

Three small ideas, each one closing a gap the previous one leaves open.

1 · Gate

O(1) plausibility rules discard noise and flag physically implausible readings (temperature jump, gas alarm, PIR flooding). Only events — plus a heartbeat so that silence is evidence — are logged.

2 · Seal

hi = SHA256(seq|ts|event|value|hi−1)
mi = HMAC(Ki, hi)
Ki+1 = SHA256(Ki)

Editing a stone breaks every link after it; the MAC stops anyone who can recompute hashes; the ratchet protects the past if the device is stolen later.

3 · Verify offline

tools/verify.py replays the log with K₀ and an optional anchor (seq, head hash), and localises the first altered record.

Honest status

Verified

Hash chain, HMAC, ratchet and verifier are unit-tested on a host (16 tests). The firmware boots in Wokwi, syncs time, and logs events such as GAS_ALARM.

Not claimed

Never run on real ESP32 hardware. No log rotation, flash-wear management or secure key storage. Detection rates come from synthetic host experiments.
